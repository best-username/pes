# SockLock: business context for ad copy

Read this before writing any variant. Facts in **Product** come from the business brief; everything
in **Audience** is a working assumption the creative team should replace with their own research.

## Product
- Magnetic socks that do not get lost in the washing machine: a small magnet on the cuffs holds the pair together.
- Subscription: five pairs for $29, then a new delivery every quarter.
- Main market: USA. Small tests in the UK, Canada and Australia (English only).
- Funnel: ad creative → web quiz → personal offer → payment. The ad's job is the click into the quiz,
  so the CTA invites the viewer to take the quiz, it does not sell the price.

## Audience (assumption)
- Parents doing the family laundry; people who share a washing machine; anyone tired of odd socks.
- Pains, in their own words: "the washer eats socks", "a drawer full of singles", "matching socks
  after every wash", "buying new packs every few months".

## How a modification differs from a new script
A winner already proved its pain, angle and promise. A modification keeps them and changes one
element, so the test shows whether that element moves the click. Write what the new element does
(a sharper first second, a different on-screen phrasing), not what it avoids.

## Copy rules
- Spoken English, short words, second person.
- Promise only what the product does: the pair stays together through the wash. No guarantees,
  health claims, fake scarcity or questions about personal attributes (Meta ad policy).
- Hook: the first ~3 seconds of voiceover, at most 10 words.
- On-screen text: at most 40 characters per line; separate lines with `|`.
